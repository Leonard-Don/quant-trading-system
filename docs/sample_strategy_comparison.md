# 多策略对照回放报告

## Why this report exists

The repo ships three ETF strategies — `EtfRotationStrategy`,
`EtfMeanReversionStrategy`, and `EtfStrategyBlend` — but each is tested
in isolation. Without a head-to-head reading on the **same** window /
universe / rebalance cadence, the user can only A/B/C them manually.
This report (generated by `scripts/compare_strategies.py`, driven by
`src/backtest/strategy_comparison.py`) closes that loop: every strategy
goes through the same `EtfRotationBacktester` engine over `2024-01-01
→ 2025-04-30` (the same window the walkforward analyzer uses) and the
output ranks each by Sharpe / total return / Calmar / max-DD / turnover,
splits the period at the midpoint into trending vs. choppy halves, and
emits the pairwise spreads so a UI can render "blend beat rotation by
+X pp" tags without re-doing the subtraction.

## Honest reading

- **Sharpe winner: `blend` (0.654)** — beats rotation (0.624) and MR
  (0.624) on risk-adjusted return, by a thin margin. The win is real
  but not huge.
- **Total-return winner: `rotation` (+8.71%)** — pure trend captured
  the most absolute upside on this window, but at the cost of the
  biggest drawdown (-8.59%) and the highest turnover (7.55%). All three
  still **trail equal-weight buy-and-hold (+23.55%)** over the same
  window — the entire rotation family is, on this period, a
  *drawdown-control* play, not an alpha generator.
- **Calmar winner: `blend` (0.878)** — the regime-aware mix lands the
  highest return-per-unit-of-drawdown, validating the textbook
  "ensembles smooth single-regime failure" thesis on this tape.
- **Lowest max-DD: `mean_reversion` (6.03%)** vs. rotation's 8.59% —
  consistent with MR's design (gated by long-trend, refuses to chase
  rallies that have already extended).
- **Lowest turnover: `blend` (5.22%)** — the linear combination
  reduces churn because trend / MR signals partially offset each
  other instead of double-counting a single re-entry.

## Insight: regime separation is *real* on this window

The midpoint split (regime detector at `_linear_fit_r2(...)`) tags
**first-half** (Jan-Aug 2024, R^2=0.370) as **choppy** and
**second-half** (Sep 2024-Apr 2025, R^2=0.792) as **trending**.
Per-strategy half-returns:

- `rotation` posts +3.69% in trending, **+5.48% in choppy** — but the
  choppy-half edge is mostly from a single late-window rally inside the
  half, not from genuinely thriving in sideways tape.
- `mean_reversion` posts **+6.17% in trending**, -0.47% in choppy. MR
  benefits when the long-trend gate stays open and dips come back;
  flat/choppy tape gives it nothing to catch.
- `blend` lands between the two in both halves (+4.99% / +2.49%),
  which is exactly what a regime-aware mix *should* do: smooth the
  per-regime extremes without giving up too much edge in either.

So yes — there is clear regime separation. Rotation and MR are
**genuinely complementary** on this window (trending winner = MR,
choppy winner = rotation, opposite halves). The blend captures most of
each child's strength while clipping the worst-case drawdown.

## Caveats inherited from v0.1

Every per-strategy backtest used the v0.1 single-strategy engine, which
**does not model** transaction costs, bid-ask spread, slippage, market
impact, execution delay beyond the one-bar lag, or survivorship bias.
The comparison is *internally* apples-to-apples (all three strategies
ate the same simplifications), but absolute numbers are not
cost-adjusted. With a typical CN ETF 5–10 bps round-trip fee, the
turnover ranking (rotation 7.55% > MR 6.05% > blend 5.22%) means
rotation's headline +8.71% would shrink first and blend's lead would
widen.

## Bottom line

The blender is **not** "just the average" of trend + MR on this window
— it posts the best Sharpe (+0.030 vs rotation) and best Calmar (+0.09)
*and* the lowest turnover. The ranking would likely widen once
realistic execution costs are layered on. Pure rotation still has the
absolute-return edge, MR has the drawdown edge, and the blend keeps
80%+ of each strength.
